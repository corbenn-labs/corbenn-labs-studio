## Executive Summary

**Industry consensus (2025–2026):** For a developing independent artist prioritizing **sustainable long-term audience growth**, the strongest evidence-supported cadence is **one single every 4–6 weeks** (roughly 8–10 release moments per year), or **every 6–8 weeks** when production and marketing capacity is limited. **Quarterly (12-week) gaps** sit at the outer edge of algorithmic relevance for artists still building an audience. **Biweekly (every 2 weeks)** is generally associated with diminishing returns, promotion cannibalization, and unsustainable workload for small teams.

**Best-fit model for a completed album:** The **waterfall release** — singles spaced 4–8 weeks apart, each with its own promotional window, culminating in a full album that consolidates prior streams via consistent ISRCs — is the most frequently cited strategy for independent artists with finished catalogs (CD Baby, UnitedMasters, LANDR, Orphiq, Chartlex).

**Platform behavior (verified official + multi-source):**
- **Spotify:** Each pitched release (≥7 days before release, ideally 21–28 days) triggers Release Radar for followers, a new editorial pitch window, and algorithmic re-evaluation. This Is playlist pins last up to **28 days** post-release (Spotify Support, official).
- **YouTube / YouTube Music:** Multi-format release (long-form anchor + Shorts on release day) is officially recommended. Algorithmic cold-start requires meaningful early engagement; the **first 14 days** post-release carry disproportionate weight. Shorts and long-form do not suppress each other when used together (YouTube for Artists, official).
- **Apple Music:** Editorial pitch requires **≥10 days** before release (7-day minimum for late add). Consistent release activity supports Discovery Station and personalized radio surfacing (Velveteen.fm, Beat Cave).

**Primary marketing focus per single:** Industry sources converge on **release week plus 2–4 weeks post-release** as the core promotional window before shifting primary focus to the next release. Total cycle from one single to the next is typically **6–10 weeks** when promotion is executed properly (Music.co.uk, NotNoise, Orphiq, Nashville Music Consultants).

**Corbenn Labs operational context (verified — repository):**

**Evidence-based recommendation for the next 12 months:** Adopt a **6-week single cadence** using a **waterfall rollout** of the four remaining album tracks, with a **full-album capstone** in month 11–12 — **conditional on** completing presence infrastructure (OAC, long-form anchor, cross-linking) before accelerating DSP releases beyond the current *El Cambio de Tono* campaign window. This cadence balances algorithmic frequency (6–9 cycles/year), Corbenn Labs' single-operator production capacity, and the approved depth-first YouTube marketing model.

### Question 1 — What release cadence produces the strongest long-term audience growth for a new independent artist?

**Comparison of cadence options** (synthesized from multiple independent industry sources, 2025–2026):

| Cadence | Releases/year | Evidence summary | Long-term growth fit |
|---------|---------------|------------------|----------------------|
| **Every 2 weeks** | ~26 | Associated with promotion cannibalization, insufficient pitch/pre-save windows (min. 4 weeks between releases cited by MusicPulse), artist burnout, and diminishing returns above ~10 releases/year (MusicPulse 2025 analysis). Orphiq: "dumping songs into the void" without per-release marketing. | **Weak** for small teams and story-led brands |
| **Every 4 weeks** | ~13 | Cited as upper bound of optimal range; works when catalog is pre-finished and promotional team/assets exist (LANDR, Chartlex, Orphiq). Risk of rushed marketing without automation. | **Strong** if capacity proven; **moderate risk** for single-operator workflows |
| **Every 5–6 weeks** | 8–10 | Most frequent "sweet spot" in 2025–2026 guides: balances Release Radar frequency, editorial pitch lead time (21–28 days), and post-release promotion (Streaming Calculator, Chartlex 2026, LANDR, MusicPulse, Beat Cave). | **Strongest consensus** for developing independents |
| **Every 8 weeks** | 6–7 | Described as sustainable default when production or promotion capacity is limited (Orphiq, Music Money Makeover, Nashville Music Consultants). Still maintains majority of algorithmic benefit. Rock/indie genres often cited toward slower end (Orphiq: 8–12 weeks). | **Strong** for quality- and story-first artists |
| **Quarterly (12 weeks)** | 4 | Orphiq: "outer edge before losing algorithmic momentum." Artists releasing less than every 10–12 weeks tend to maintain weaker algorithmic presence. Viable for established catalogs with existing audience; weaker for cold-start growth. | **Moderate to weak** for new independent artists building audience |

**Fact (Spotify official):** Pitching ≥7 days before release guarantees Release Radar inclusion for followers; only one song can be pitched at a time until it goes live (Spotify Support).

**Observation:** No major platform publishes a single official "optimal cadence" number. Consensus emerges from distributor guides, artist-education publishers, and aggregated campaign analyses — not from platform policy documents alone.

**Industry consensus answer:** **Every 5–6 weeks** produces the strongest *general* long-term growth balance for new independents. **Every 8 weeks** is the best-supported slower alternative. **Every 2 weeks** and **quarterly** are outliers at opposite ends — too fast for promotion depth, too slow for algorithmic momentum during audience-building phases.

### Question 2 — How do Spotify and YouTube algorithms respond to different release frequencies?

#### Spotify (and by extension YouTube Music via distributor delivery)

| Mechanism | Response to frequency | Source |
|-----------|----------------------|--------|
| **Release Radar** | Triggered per eligible pitched release (≥7 days lead). More spaced releases = more annual "new music" moments for followers. | Spotify Support (official) |
| **Editorial playlists** | One pitch per release; 21–28 days lead recommended for editorial review time (MusicPulse, Unchained Music — industry; 7-day minimum official). | Spotify Support + industry |
| **This Is playlist pin** | Pinned song visible up to **28 days** post-release. Faster cadence (<4 weeks) can overlap pin windows and split follower attention. | Spotify Support (official) |
| **Discover Weekly / algorithmic playlists** | Reward sustained engagement (saves, completion, repeat listens) — not raw upload volume (Orphiq, Chartlex). Chartlex: algorithmic playlists drove ~31% more discovery than editorial in analyzed campaigns. | Chartlex 2026 |
| **Too fast** | Cannibalizes promotional cycles; lower save rates; weaker per-track signals (MusicPulse, Nashville Music Consultants). | Industry |
| **Too slow** | Fewer data points for listener profiling; reduced Release Radar frequency; audience attention decay after ~10–12 weeks (Orphiq). | Industry |

#### YouTube (artist channel + YouTube Music)

| Mechanism | Response to frequency | Source |
|-----------|----------------------|--------|
| **Multi-format release** | Official recommendation: Shorts + long-form (MV/visualizer) on/near release day; formats cross-influence recommendations without mutual suppression. | YouTube for Artists (official) |
| **Recency weighting** | First **14 days** post-release disproportionately influence recommendations; first 24–48 hours critical for long-form watch signals (Chartlex, NotNoise). | Industry |
| **Cold-start threshold** | YouTube Music typically needs **100–500 full listens** with strong completion before broad algorithmic surfacing; smaller artists may need 30–60 days organically (Chartlex 2026). | Industry |
| **Channel consistency** | Consistent posting rhythm (e.g., weekly long-form + several Shorts) preferred over erratic bursts; "consistency matters more than volume" (Chartlex 2026). | Industry |
| **Release extension** | Supplementary content (lyric video, BTS, acoustic) in weeks 2–4 extends algorithmic life of a single (NotNoise, YouTube for Artists). | Industry + official |

**Observation:** Spotify rewards **discrete release events** spaced enough for engagement signals to mature (~2–4 weeks). YouTube rewards **sustained content activity around each release** more than DSP upload frequency alone. For Corbenn Labs' approved YouTube-hub model, YouTube content cadence and DSP release cadence should be coordinated — not treated independently.

### Question 3 — How long should a single remain the primary marketing focus before releasing the next song?

**Pre-release phase (next single):**

| Activity | Lead time | Source |
|----------|-----------|--------|
| Spotify editorial pitch | 7 days minimum; **21–28 days** recommended | Spotify Support; MusicPulse; Unchained Music |
| Apple Music editorial pitch | **10 days** minimum (7 late add) | Velveteen.fm |
| Distributor delivery | 2–4 weeks before release | NotNoise; Stephen Allen Music Group |
| Pre-save / pre-add campaigns | 2–3 weeks before release | PUSH.fm; Stephen Allen Music Group |
| YouTube Shorts tease | 2–4 weeks before; 2–3 Shorts/week | NotNoise; YouTube for Artists |

**Primary focus window (current single):**

| Phase | Duration | Activities | Source |
|-------|----------|------------|--------|
| Release week | Days 1–7 | Premiere/long-form launch, Shorts cross-post, fan activation, pitch confirmation | NotNoise; YouTube for Artists |
| Early post-release | Days 8–14 | Highest algorithmic weight; sustain engagement | Chartlex |
| Extended post-release | Weeks 2–4 | Supplementary content, playlist follow-up, learnings capture | NotNoise; Orphiq; Music.co.uk |
| Transition to next single | Week 4–6 | Plan next release; begin pre-release tease while current track enters catalog mode | Music.co.uk (6–10 week total cycle) |

**Facts:**
- Spotify This Is pin duration: up to **28 days** (official).
- Nashville Music Consultants: "window of momentum" typically **2–3 weeks** post-release for algorithmic evaluation.
- Orphiq waterfall guide: initial algorithmic evaluation **2–3 weeks** per single.

**Consensus answer:** A single should remain the **primary marketing focus for approximately 4–6 weeks total** (including 2–3 weeks pre-release for the *next* single overlapping the tail of the current one). Minimum credible promotional window: **release week + 2–4 weeks post-release** before shifting primary audience messaging to the next chapter.

### Question 4 — What strategies are currently used by successful independent artists who already have a completed album?

**Documented strategies (external industry + Corbenn Labs verified comparables):**

| Strategy | Description | Evidence |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **Waterfall / stacked singles** | Release singles sequentially; each new product bundles prior tracks with identical ISRCs; streams consolidate toward final album/EP. | CD Baby (official); UnitedMasters Help Center; LabelGrid; PUSH.fm |
| **Album-era / chapter framing** | Each single positioned as part of a larger narrative, not a disposable drop. | Noche Cero, Blucer (verified in ECT `research-report.md`); aligns with Corbenn Chapter model |
| **Singles-first, album capstone** | 4–6 singles over 6–12 months, then full album release. | LANDR; Chartlex 2026; Orphiq |
| **Pre-release social buildup** | Short-form narrative content before DSP date to warm algorithm and audience. | Noche Cero model (ECT research); NotNoise 2026 timeline |
| **Per-release artwork** | Unique cover per single; album art reserved for capstone. | CD Baby; LabelGrid |
| **Per-release editorial pitch** | Pitch strongest tracks selectively if releasing faster than monthly; pitch all if monthly or slower. | Dynamoi; Spotify Support |
| **YouTube multi-format hub** | Long-form anchor + Shorts funnel per chapter; supplementary videos extend single lifespan. | YouTube for Artists; Corbenn approved Option A/C |
| **Friday releases** | Align with New Music Friday editorial refresh (Spotify, Apple). | Multiple industry sources; Spotify New Music Friday documentation |

**Waterfall spacing (industry):** CD Baby and LabelGrid recommend **≥1 month** between waterfall singles; LANDR and PressedFresh recommend **4–6 weeks**; PUSH.fm examples use ~4-week spacing.

**Observation:** Artists with completed albums rarely dump full projects at once in 2025–2026 independent guidance. The dominant pattern is **staggered singles → consolidated album**.

### Question 5 — What are the advantages and disadvantages of releasing faster versus slower?

#### Faster releases (every 2–4 weeks)

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|------------|---------------|
| More Release Radar / editorial pitch cycles per year | Promotion windows overlap; none reach full maturity |
| More algorithmic "shots on goal" | Higher burnout risk for small teams (Music Money Makeover 2026) |
| Maintains top-of-mind for algorithms trained on recency | Lower save/completion rates if tracks aren't fully promoted (MusicPulse) |
| Works when catalog is pre-finished | Weakens story depth per chapter — conflicts with Corbenn Brand DNA |
| Can suit high-volume genres (hip-hop, electronic) | Rock/indie/singer-songwriter audiences tolerate slower cadence (Orphiq) |

#### Slower releases (every 8–12 weeks / quarterly)

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|------------|---------------|
| Deeper promotion per release; higher engagement quality | Fewer algorithmic cycles (4 vs. 8–10 per year) |
| More time for long-form + Shorts production per chapter | Risk of audience attention decay >10–12 weeks (Orphiq) |
| Aligns with cinematic/story-led brand positioning | Weaker for cold-start artists without existing fanbase |
| Sustainable for single-operator workflows | Full album delay extends monetization timeline |
| Reduces distributor/pitch administrative load | Editorial teams favor demonstrably active artists (MusicPulse) |

**Fact:** MusicPulse 2025 analysis cites diminishing returns above ~10 releases/year and optimal range of **6–9 releases/year** (~5–7 week spacing).

**Observation:** For Corbenn Labs specifically, the approved marketing system already biases toward **slower content depth** (long-form anchor, minimal Shorts volume). Evidence suggests DSP cadence should not outpace YouTube/marketing capacity — otherwise releases enter "catalog mode" without sufficient discovery support.

## Industry Consensus

1. **Singles-first, album-later** outperform single-album-dump for developing independents (LANDR, Chartlex, CD Baby, Orphiq — convergent).
2. **4–6 weeks** between singles is the most cited optimal range; **6–8 weeks** is the sustainable alternative.
3. **Waterfall releases** are the standard method for artists with completed albums who want stream consolidation and repeated algorithmic entry points.
4. **Each release requires its own promotional window** (~4–6 weeks total cycle); faster drops without marketing depth underperform.
5. **Friday releases** remain industry default for Spotify/Apple editorial alignment.
6. **YouTube and Spotify should be coordinated** — DSP frequency should not exceed the team's ability to ship long-form anchor + story Shorts per chapter.
7. **Consistency beats bursts** — predictable cadence outperforms irregular high-volume drops followed by silence (Orphiq, Beat Cave).
